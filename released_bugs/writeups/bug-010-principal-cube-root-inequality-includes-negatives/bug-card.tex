\section*{Bug 10: principal cube-root inequality includes negatives}

\begin{counterexamplebox}
\textbf{Task.} Solve the inequality over real \(x\) as represented in SymPy by
\texttt{x**(S(1)/3) <= 1}.
\[
    x^{1/3} \leq 1
\]

\medskip
\textbf{SymPy's answer.}
\[
   (-\infty,1].
\]

\medskip
\textbf{Correct answer.}
\[
    [0,1].
\]

\medskip
\textbf{Explanation.} The expression \texttt{x**(S(1)/3)} is a principal
\texttt{Pow}, not SymPy's real-root operation. Thus, unlike the real cube root,
SymPy's principal value at \(x=-1\) is
\[
    (-1)^{1/3}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}i,
\]
so the ordered real comparison with \(1\) is not satisfied there. On the
real-valued domain \(x\geq0\), the principal cube root is monotone and
\(x^{1/3}\leq1\) gives \(0\leq x\leq1\).

\medskip
\textbf{Likely root cause.} The diagnosis points to
\texttt{continuous\_domain} treating odd-denominator fractional powers as
real-valued over all real bases, which matches \texttt{real\_root} semantics
rather than principal \texttt{Pow} semantics; the inequality solver then samples
and accepts an interval containing negative inputs. Deduplication frames this
as a new instance of a known failure mode.

\medskip
\textbf{Artifact (GitHub).} \bugbundle{bug-010-principal-cube-root-inequality-includes-negatives}
\end{counterexamplebox}
