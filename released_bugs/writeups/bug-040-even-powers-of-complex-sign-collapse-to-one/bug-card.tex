\section*{Bug 40: Even Powers of Complex Sign Collapse to One}

\begin{counterexamplebox}
\textbf{Task.} Evaluate an even power of the complex sign at a non-real argument:
\[
\operatorname{sign}(1+i)^2.
\]

\medskip
\textbf{SymPy's answer.}
\[
\operatorname{sign}(1+i)^2 = 1.
\]

\medskip
\textbf{Correct answer.}
\[
\operatorname{sign}(1+i)^2
= \frac{(1+i)^2}{|1+i|^2}
= i.
\]

\medskip
\textbf{Explanation.} For nonzero complex \(z\), complex sign is the unit direction \(z/|z|\). Substituting \(z=1+i\) gives \((1+i)^2/2=i\), so the returned real value \(1\) is not mathematically equivalent.

\medskip
\textbf{Likely root cause.} The diagnosis points to \texttt{sign.\_eval\_power} in \nolinkurl{sympy/functions/elementary/complexes.py}, where an even-integer power of any nonzero \texttt{sign} expression is simplified to \(1\) without requiring a real argument. This is a new instance of a known failure mode.

\medskip
\textbf{Artifact (GitHub).} \bugbundle{bug-040-even-powers-of-complex-sign-collapse-to-one}
\end{counterexamplebox}
