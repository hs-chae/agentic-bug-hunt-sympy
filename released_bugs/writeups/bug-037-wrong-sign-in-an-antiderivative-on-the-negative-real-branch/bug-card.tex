\section*{Bug 37: Wrong Sign in an Antiderivative on the Negative Real Branch}

\begin{counterexamplebox}
\textbf{Task.} Compute an antiderivative of the algebraic integrand
\[
    \int \dfrac{dx}{x\sqrt{x^2-1}} .
\]

\medskip
\textbf{SymPy's answer.} SymPy returns
\[
    \operatorname{integrate}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x\sqrt{x^2-1}},x\right)
    =
    \begin{cases}
    i\,\operatorname{acosh}(1/x), & 1/|x^2|>1,\\
    -\operatorname{asin}(1/x), & \text{otherwise.}
    \end{cases}
\]

\medskip
\textbf{Correct answer.} On the negative real branch \(x<-1\), the derivative
of the returned antiderivative should be
\[
    \dfrac{1}{x\sqrt{x^2-1}} .
\]

\medskip
\textbf{Explanation.} At \(x=-3\), the integrand is approximately
\(-0.11785113019775792\), but differentiating SymPy's fallback branch
\(-\operatorname{asin}(1/x)\) gives \(+0.11785113019775792\).  The fallback derivative is
\(1/(|x|\sqrt{x^2-1})\), which is correct on \(x>1\) but has the opposite sign
on \(x<-1\).

\medskip
\textbf{Likely root cause.} The diagnosis narrows the issue to the Meijer-G
indefinite integration path in \nolinkurl{sympy/integrals/meijerint.py}, where a
rewrite, hyperexpansion, polar power denesting, and unpolarification sequence
appears to collapse the \(x>1\) and \(x<-1\) branches into one
\(-\operatorname{asin}(1/x)\) fallback.  This is a new instance of a known failure mode;
the same \(\sqrt{x^2-1}\) integration family appears in public issues
\href{https://github.com/sympy/sympy/issues/20982}{sympy/sympy\#20982}
(``lost sign when \(x<0\)'') and
\href{https://github.com/sympy/sympy/issues/23566}{sympy/sympy\#23566}
(the \(\operatorname{acosh}/\operatorname{asin}\) branch handling), but neither
reports the exact \(1/(x\sqrt{x^2-1})\) indefinite sign error.

\medskip

\end{counterexamplebox}
