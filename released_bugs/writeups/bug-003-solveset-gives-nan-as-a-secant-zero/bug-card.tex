\section*{Bug 3: solveset gives \texttt{nan} as a secant zero}

\begin{counterexamplebox}
\textbf{Task.} Solve the secant equation over the complex domain:
\[
    \sec(x)=0.
\]

\medskip
\textbf{SymPy's answer.}
\[
    \operatorname{solveset}(\sec(x),\,x,\,\mathbb{C})=\{\mathtt{nan}\}.
\]

\medskip
\textbf{Correct answer.}
\[
    \varnothing.
\]

\medskip
\textbf{Explanation.} For finite complex \(z\), \(\sec(z)=1/\cos(z)\) is never
zero. The reported candidate is not a complex solution;
\(\sec(\mathtt{nan})=\mathtt{nan}\), not \(0\).

\medskip
\textbf{Likely root cause.} The diagnosis traces the result to
\texttt{\_invert\_trig\_hyp\_complex} in
\nolinkurl{sympy/solvers/solveset.py}, which applies \texttt{asec(0)} while
inverting \(\sec(x)=0\) and lacks a reciprocal-trig range guard excluding zero.
This is a new instance of a known failure mode.

\medskip
\textbf{Artifact (GitHub).} \bugbundle{bug-003-solveset-gives-nan-as-a-secant-zero}
\end{counterexamplebox}
