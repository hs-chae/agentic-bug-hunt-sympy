\section*{Bug 9: finite product masks an undefined factor}

\begin{counterexamplebox}
\textbf{Task.} Evaluate the finite product
\[
\prod_{n=0}^{2}\frac{\sin(\pi n)}{n-1}.
\]

\medskip
\textbf{SymPy's answer.} \(\operatorname{product}(\sin(\pi n)/(n-1),(n,0,2))=0\).

\medskip
\textbf{Correct answer.} The product is \(\mathrm{nan}\), or should remain
unevaluated.

\medskip
\textbf{Explanation.} At \(n=1\), the factor is \(\sin(\pi)/(1-1)=0/0\),
so the product contains an undefined term. Hence the finite product is not
equal to \(0\), even though the factors at \(n=0\) and \(n=2\) vanish.

\medskip
\textbf{Likely root cause.} In \texttt{Product.doit}, SymPy replaces the index
by an integer-assumption dummy before evaluating the finite product, so
\(\sin(\pi n)\) collapses to \(0\) and masks the denominator singularity at
\(n=1\). This is a new failure mode with no public precedent.

\medskip
\textbf{Artifact (GitHub).} \bugbundle{bug-009-finite-product-masks-an-undefined-factor}
\end{counterexamplebox}
