\section*{Bug 54: singularities misses the pole of gamma(x) at zero}

\begin{counterexamplebox}
\textbf{Task.} Find the complex singularities of the gamma function.
\[
\operatorname{singularities}(\Gamma(x), x, \mathbb{C})
\]

\medskip
\textbf{SymPy's answer.}
\[
\operatorname{singularities}(\Gamma(x), x, \mathbb{C})=\emptyset
\]

\medskip
\textbf{Correct answer.} The result must contain \(0\); in fact, \(\Gamma(x)\) has finite poles at \(0,-1,-2,\ldots\).

\medskip
\textbf{Explanation.} SymPy itself evaluates \(\Gamma(0)\) as complex infinity, computes residue \(1\) at \(0\), and gives the Laurent expansion \(\Gamma(x)=1/x-\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}+O(x)\). The nonzero \(1/x\) term proves a genuine pole, so \(\emptyset\) is mathematically false.

\medskip
\textbf{Likely root cause.} The scanner in \nolinkurl{sympy/calculus/singularities.py} handles powers and a small list of logarithmic or inverse-hyperbolic atoms, but it has no path for finite poles of \(\Gamma(g(x))\) and does not use gamma's numeric pole knowledge. This is a new failure mode with no public precedent.

\medskip
\textbf{Artifact (GitHub).} \bugbundle{bug-054-singularities-misses-the-pole-of-gamma-x-at-zero}
\end{counterexamplebox}
