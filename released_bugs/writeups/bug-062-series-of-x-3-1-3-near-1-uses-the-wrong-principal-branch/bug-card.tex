\section*{Bug 62: Series of \texorpdfstring{\((x^3)^{1/3}\)}{(x**3)**(1/3)} Near \texorpdfstring{\(-1\)}{-1} Uses the Wrong Principal Branch}

\begin{counterexamplebox}
\textbf{Task.} Expand the principal cube root near a negative real point.
\[
\operatorname{series}\!\left((x^3)^{1/3}, x, -1, 2\right)
\]

\medskip
\textbf{SymPy's answer.}
\[
\operatorname{series}\!\left((x^3)^{1/3}, x, -1, 2\right)=x
\]

\medskip
\textbf{Correct answer.}
\[
\lim_{x\to -1}(x^3)^{1/3}=e^{i\pi/3}\neq -1
\]

\medskip
\textbf{Explanation.} For negative real \(x\) near \(-1\), \(x^3\) is negative real, so the principal cube root has argument \(\pi/3\).  Thus \((x^3)^{1/3}=(-x)e^{i\pi/3}\) on that side, while SymPy's series \(x\) gives the real value \(-1\) at the center.

\medskip
\textbf{Likely root cause.} In \nolinkurl{sympy/core/power.py}, \texttt{Pow.\_eval\_nseries()} applies \texttt{powdenest(force=True)}, which rewrites \texttt{((t - 1)**3)**(1/3)} to \texttt{t - 1} after shifting the expansion point.  That forced denesting loses the principal-branch phase; this is a new instance of a known failure mode.

\medskip
\textbf{Artifact (GitHub).} \bugbundle{bug-062-series-of-x-3-1-3-near-1-uses-the-wrong-principal-branch}
\end{counterexamplebox}
